Its ya boi Elder Miller back with another weekly email. Make sure to like,

Ok not really a scripture but a Bible Dictionary entry. You know, the part
of your quad you always accidentally turn to but never actually look at? I
read it. It was pretty dope. I found some pretty neat stuff in there. I'm
not saying you should read it because like actually reading scripture is
probably better but it was pretty neat nonetheless. I didn't know this but
I'm pretty sure Bruce R. McConkie (an apostle in the '80s(??)) and his son
wrote a fair portion of the dictionary. This is from Meta AI though so take
that with a grain of salt.
OK ANYWAY THE ENTRY IN QUESTION:
A temple is literally a house of the Lord, a holy sanctuary in which sacred
ceremonies and ordinances of the gospel are performed by and for the living
and also in behalf of the dead. A place where the Lord may come, it is the
most holy of any place of worship on the earth. *Only the home can compare
with the temple in sacredness.*
Bars. It's a pretty standard definition for a temple. They're very
important places of worship where we make sacred covenants with our
Heavenly Father. We also perform these ordinances on behalf of those who
have passed. Then, that last line comes in. Only the home can compare in
sacredness. Phew! That's so cool to me. It doesn't say the chapels, nor the
sacred grove, nor anything else. The home is the ONLY thing that can
compare to the temple, but only if we let it. So how do we let it? Well,
first off, cleanliness invites the spirit. If you're struggling to feel the
spirit in your studies at home, try cleaning up a bit. The house of the
Lord is a house of order (D&C 88:119). Your house should be one, too. I
know that as I've been out here on my mission, cleaning my apartment has
made a huge difference on my ability to feel the spirit. The second thing
is to concecrste your house. What does that mean? Most of your studies
should be happening in the home. If you aren't studying enough, study more!
The Lord will bless your efforts and your home as you treat it more like a
temple. Ok that was a lot my bad.
