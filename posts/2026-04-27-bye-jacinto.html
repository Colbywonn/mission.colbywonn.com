Anyway, scripture time:

Let's talk Sabbath Day. Not what day it is, because frankly that is not a
discussion I want to get into right now. Just what it means to keep it
holy. Recently, a bunch of friends and recent converts have asked us why we
we wear shirts and ties and skirts and dresses to church. I realized that
in my entire mission, I had always taught keeping the sabbath day holy
basically as just going to church. I failed to capture the scope of what is
truly is for, and how keeping it holy can benefit us. Jesus Christ is the
Lord of the Sabbath, and it is a day that He has set apart for us. In
Spanish, the Sabbath is referred to as "el día de reposo" or the day of
rest. When Jehovah created the heavens and the Earth, He rested from his
labors on the 7th and last day of crestion. Just like with everything he
did and does, it was an example for us. Sunday is a day to change the
course of our weeks. It lets us take an account of our spiritual state, and
renovate it. With that in mind, the activities we choose to do or don't
choose to do are pretty expansive. I love what President Nelson had to say
about it:
"How do we hallow the Sabbath day? In my much younger years, I studied the
work of others who had compiled lists of things to do and things not to do
on the Sabbath. It wasn’t until later that I learned from the scriptures
that my conduct and my attitude on the Sabbath constituted a sign between
me and my Heavenly Father. With that understanding, I no longer needed
lists of dos and don’ts. When I had to make a decision whether or not an
activity was appropriate for the Sabbath, I simply asked myself, “What sign
do I want to give to God?” That question made my choices about the Sabbath
day crystal clear."
The sign we send to God should be things we do on Sunday. And probably
every other day, too haha!
